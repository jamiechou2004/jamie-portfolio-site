01 / The problem

Furnishing a home is harder
than choosing a product.

1 in 9Americans moved in 2024Point2Homes / Census, via Axios, 2025

Hard to picture what fits

Product photos show other people’s rooms.

Hard to add up the total

Every piece has its own site, cart and fees.

Hard to change one piece

One swap can mean redoing the plan.

03 / Research

Plans don’t fail in the store.
They fail at the door.

Doorways

Shoppers forget to measure the way in.

Shopwise asks for door and elevator size before checkout.

Measure first

Nothing is final until the room is measured.

Shopwise starts every plan from your room size.

Plus two usability sessions on wireframes

Two moderated sessions (P1, P2) on the first wireframes. What they changed is below.

04 / What testing changed

Two sessions.
Three fixes.

Every charge listed

P2 saw a total over budget and couldn’t tell why. Now each cost has its own line, with what’s left of the budget.

Replace sits on the item

P2 couldn’t find where to swap a piece. Replace moved onto the selected item.

Add to plan, on the item

P1 liked a piece but had no way to add it. Add to plan now sits on the item.

10 / Reflection

The hardest AI decision
is where it should stop.

Decide where AI stops

Most of the work was choosing what the assistant must not do: guess sizes, hide doubt, or spend money.

Uncertainty can build trust

Marking an estimate and making it editable felt more trustworthy than a confident wrong answer.
