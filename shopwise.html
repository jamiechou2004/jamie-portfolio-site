01 — The problem

People don’t know how to make a new place feel like home.

  • 1Hard to picture what fitsNo picture of the finished room.
  • 2Hard to add up the totalEvery piece lives in a different store.
  • 3Hard to change one pieceSo guesses go wrong.

02 — The user

Alex just moved to New York.

  • Just graduated
  • First job in New York
  • Lives alone
  • Empty apartment, about $3,000
How might wehelp someone who just moved turn scattered ideas into one plan that fits their space, their life and their budget, and stays easy to change?

03 — Research

Two experts showed us how the whole system works.

Interior designer

  • Measure on site before anything is final
  • Photos can’t show how people really live
  • One missing piece means redoing the plan

IKEA store staff

  • Shoppers miss doorways, elevators, outlets
  • Showrooms make size and color look different
  • Delivery and assembly hand-offs get lost

So we designed one digital ecosystem.

Low-fi first. Then testing changed three things.

    The plan cost more than the budget, and the participant couldn’t tell why.

    Replace sits on the item.

    Liked a piece, but had no way to add it.

08 — Final experience

Two sides of one plan.

CustomerAlex · iPhone and Apple WatchOnboarding · Design a room · Change a piece · Checkout · Delivery and after
GMOperations · web consoleFollow one order: see it, fix it, close it

Flow 1 · Onboarding

Sign up and land on a plan.

Why it worksHome opens with the plan ready to continue. In testing, P2 had to search for where it was.

Flow 2 · Design a room

A room brief in taps, not forms.

Why it worksSpace limits are asked next to style, then shown in one brief you can edit. Both experts said those come first.

Flow 3 · Change a piece

Swap one item, keep the room.

Why it worksReplace sits on the selected item, and trying is separate from keeping. P2 said the old control was too far from the image.

Flow 4 · Checkout

One order, one clear total.

Why it worksEvery cost and what is left of the budget are shown before paying. P2 questioned the gap.

Flow 5 · Delivery and after

A problem becomes an exchange.

Why it worksCheck-in offers three answers, so coming back does not have to mean a return. The watch handles arrival and rating.

Shoppal · AI assistant

A sofa, shown in your own room.

Why it worksThe wall size is marked as an estimate you can fix, and the assistant stops at Add to plan. It never buys.

The other side

The GM keeps Alex’s order on track: one web console, one order across three stores.

Flow 6 · For the manager

See it, fix it, close it.

Why it worksOne late bed holds a delivery from three stores. The manager gets order, crew, message and new slot in one place.

10 — Reflection

The hardest AI decision is where it should stop.

Learned

Decide where AI stops

Most of the work was choosing what the assistant must not do: guess sizes, hide doubt, or spend money.

Decided

Honest estimates earn trust

Show an estimate you can fix, not a confident answer that might be wrong.

Next

Build it for real
